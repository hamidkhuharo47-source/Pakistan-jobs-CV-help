Interview Preparation

Prepare clear, truthful examples and practise explaining your skills.

Before the interview

  • Research the organisation and role.
  • Read the job description and identify required skills.
  • Prepare examples from study, work, volunteering or projects.
  • Plan your travel or test your video-call setup.
  • Carry copies of your CV and relevant documents if requested.
  • Dress appropriately and arrive on time.

Use the STAR method

Describe the Situation, Task, Action and Result. Keep answers focused and truthful. If you do not know an answer, explain how you would find out.

5 common interview questions with sample answers

1. Tell me about yourself.

Sample answer: "I have completed my education in [your qualification]. I am interested in this position because it matches my skills and career goals. I am responsible, willing to learn and motivated to contribute to the organisation."

2. Why do you want this job?

Sample answer: "I am interested in this role because it matches my abilities and gives me an opportunity to develop professionally. I would like to use my skills and contribute positively to your organisation."

3. What are your strengths?

Sample answer: "My strengths include punctuality, willingness to learn, communication and attention to detail. I try to complete my responsibilities carefully and on time."

4. What is your weakness?

Sample answer: "Sometimes I spend extra time checking my work because I want it to be accurate. I am learning to manage my time better while maintaining quality."

5. How do you handle a difficult task?

Sample answer: "I first understand the problem, divide it into smaller steps and identify the best way to solve it. If I need guidance, I ask the relevant person and learn from the experience."

Important: These are examples to help you prepare. Adapt each answer to your real education, experience and abilities. Do not claim skills or achievements you do not have.

How to answer "Tell me about yourself"

Keep your introduction brief and organised. You can follow this simple structure:

  1. Start with your name and educational qualification.
  2. Mention relevant skills or practical experience.
  3. Explain your interest in the position.
  4. Finish by explaining what you hope to contribute and learn.

Practise your introduction aloud so that it sounds natural rather than memorised.

Interview tips for fresh graduates

  • Discuss relevant subjects and projects from your education.
  • Mention computer skills such as MS Word, Excel or other tools only if you can use them.
  • Use examples from assignments, presentations or group projects.
  • Show that you are willing to learn and accept feedback.
  • Be honest if you do not have professional work experience.
  • Explain how your education and skills relate to the job.

Common interview mistakes to avoid

  • Arriving late without a valid reason.
  • Giving false information about qualifications or experience.
  • Interrupting the interviewer.
  • Giving answers that are too long or unrelated to the question.
  • Not researching the organisation before the interview.
  • Using your phone during the interview.
  • Speaking negatively about previous employers or teachers.

Questions you can ask the interviewer

At the end of an interview, you may be invited to ask questions. Consider asking:

  • What are the main responsibilities of this position?
  • What does a typical working day look like?
  • Will training be provided for the selected candidate?
  • What are the next steps in the recruitment process?

Final interview preparation checklist

  • Review the job description.
  • Practise your introduction and common questions.
  • Prepare your CV and requested documents.
  • Check the interview location or online meeting details.
  • Get enough rest before the interview.
  • Answer honestly and remain confident.

Good interview preparation takes practice. Review your answers, learn from each interview and continue improving your communication skills.
